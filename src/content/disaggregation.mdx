<TLDR>
  Prefill and decode want different things from a GPU, and under heavy load they get in each other's way.
  **Disaggregation** runs them on separate engines: prefill engines read prompts and build KV caches, decode engines
  write the answers. It's powerful, but only worth it for large models, lots of traffic, and long inputs.
</TLDR>

## Three ideas, combined

Disaggregation brings together three ideas from earlier lessons:

1. <Compute>Prefill</Compute> is compute-bound and sets your **TTFT**; <Memory>decode</Memory> is memory-bound and sets
   your **TPS** (see [Finding the bottleneck](/learn/bottlenecks)).
2. **Specialization improves performance**, from choosing kernels to tuning engine settings. A machine doing one job
   can be tuned for that job.
3. You can spread serving across many GPUs, or even many machines, **if you avoid bottlenecks** on the slower links
   between them.

When prefill and decode share the same GPUs under heavy traffic, they tend to **interfere**. In theory they're a good
pairing: prefill mostly uses compute, decode mostly uses memory. But with large batches and compute-hungry
optimizations, they start competing for the same resources, and a big prefill can stall everyone's token stream.

<Analogy>
  Picture a restaurant where the same cooks both **prep** (chopping for big new orders) and **cook to order**. When a
  catering order for 200 arrives, all the à-la-carte diners wait. Give prep its own team and its own station, and
  both kinds of work flow smoothly. Each team can also be sized and trained for its own job.
</Analogy>

## How disaggregation works

**Disaggregated serving** puts prefill and decode on **separate engines, on separate GPUs or machines**. A request
becomes a three-step relay:

1. The **prefill engine** reads the input, builds the KV cache, and computes the **first token**.
2. The prefill engine **sends the KV cache** over the hardware connection to a decode engine.
3. The **decode engine** generates every token after that.

### Conditional disaggregation

Shipping a KV cache has a cost, and it isn't worth paying for a tiny prompt. With **conditional disaggregation**, the
request goes to the **decode engine first**, which checks whether the input is already in its prefix cache or short
enough to handle itself:

- **If yes**, the decode engine runs prefill locally and skips disaggregation.
- **If no**, it hands the request to a prefill engine and the disaggregated relay happens.

The book notes that conditional disaggregation works **better for real-world traffic**, which is a messy mix of long
and short requests.

<DisaggFlow />

Separating the engines has another big benefit: **you can tune each one independently.** For example, the
compute-bound prefill engine needs **less tensor parallelism** than the memory-bound decode engine (see
[Using many GPUs](/learn/parallelism)).

## When should you use it?

Disaggregation needs multiple GPUs and real engineering effort. The book says to reach for it only when **all three**
of these are true:

1. **High volume**: you serve a lot of traffic, starting around **100 million to 1 billion tokens per day**, depending
   on model size.
2. **A big model**: at least around **100 billion parameters**.
3. **Prefill-heavy traffic**: many long input sequences.

If (1) or (2) is false, you're probably paying for extra hardware for little gain. If (3) is false, you may do better
using those GPUs for **more replicas**, since decode engines handle short prompts and prefix-cache hits efficiently on
their own.

The textbook workload: a **frontier LLM inside a code editor**, where many developers at once send big, varied
chunks of code as context. That's tons of tokens, mostly prefill, on a trillion-parameter model.

## Dynamic disaggregation with NVIDIA Dynamo

**NVIDIA Dynamo** provides production-ready disaggregation that can cope with messy real traffic. Its main building
blocks:

- A **prefill queue** that holds requests when every prefill engine is busy.
- Solid support for **conditional disaggregation**, with configurable thresholds for when to send a request to prefill
  engines, based on the
  <Term def="Input sequence length: how many tokens are in the prompt.">input length (ISL)</Term>
  left after prefix caching, and on how long the prefill queue is.
- Fast KV cache transfer from prefill to decode engines, built on
  <Term def="NVIDIA’s Inference Transfer Library, used by Dynamo to move KV cache data quickly between engines.">NIXL</Term>,
  including a kernel that **rearranges KV blocks** when the prefill and decode engines use different tensor-parallel
  layouts.

Together these enable **dynamic disaggregation**: the number of prefill and decode engines can be **changed at
runtime** and adjusted over time as traffic shifts.

### xPyD: not one-to-one

It's easiest to explain disaggregation with one prefill engine and one decode engine, but real systems run several
of each, in whatever ratio the traffic needs. The shorthand is **xPyD**: **5P3D** means five prefill engines and three
decode engines serving one model together.

<XPyD />

More moving parts means more places to get stuck. Disaggregation adds two new bottlenecks to watch:

- **Prefill queue size.** Don't let the queue grow too long. Set a sensible threshold so decode engines prefill
  smaller requests themselves, and reconfigure xPyD at runtime to shift engines to prefill when needed.
- **Running out of KV cache on decode engines** under heavy load. Make more room with
  [quantization](/learn/quantization) (including of the KV cache) and [KV cache offloading](/learn/caching).

<KeyPoints>
- Disaggregation splits <Compute>prefill</Compute> and <Memory>decode</Memory> onto **separate engines** so they stop interfering and can each be tuned.
- The flow: prefill engine builds the KV cache and first token → ships the cache → decode engine writes the rest.
- **Conditional disaggregation** lets decode engines handle short or cached prompts themselves, which fits real traffic best.
- Worth it only with **high volume** (~100M–1B+ tokens/day), a **big model** (100B+), and **prefill-heavy** traffic.
- **Dynamo** adds a prefill queue, routing thresholds, fast KV transfer (NIXL), and runtime-adjustable **xPyD** ratios. Watch the prefill queue and decode KV memory.
</KeyPoints>
