<TLDR>
  Image generators don't write pixels one at a time. They start with **pure static** and clean the *whole* image up a
  little on each of ~30–50 steps. Video models do the same thing, but for a whole clip at once, which makes them
  enormously expensive.
</TLDR>

## Not one model, a pipeline

An LLM is basically one big model. An image generator is a **team of models** working in sequence:

1. **Text encoder**: turns your prompt into something the image model can understand.
2. **Denoising model**: the heart of it. It turns noise into an image, guided by the prompt.
3. **VAE** (<Term def="Variational Autoencoder: converts between the compact ‘latent’ version of an image and actual pixels.">variational autoencoder</Term>):
   converts the finished result from the model's compact internal format into real pixels.

On top of that, people often bolt on extras: **LoRAs** (small add-ons that change the style) and **ControlNets**
(outlines or poses that steer the composition). Tools like ComfyUI exist just for wiring these pipelines together.

## Working in "latent space"

A 1024×1024 image has over a million pixels. Attention over every pixel would be impossibly expensive. So image
models work on a **compressed version** of the image called **latent space**, something like a 128×128 grid, about
**1% the size** of the real image. Only at the very end does the VAE blow it back up into pixels.

<Analogy>
  Like an artist working on a **small thumbnail sketch** instead of a giant canvas. All the real decisions happen on
  the thumbnail, then it gets enlarged at the end.
</Analogy>

## From noise to picture

The latent starts as random noise. On each **step**, the denoising model looks at the whole latent and nudges it to
look a bit more like "a sunset over hills" (or whatever you asked for).

<Denoise />

Some important details:

- Most models use **30 to 50 steps**.
- Each step actually runs the model **twice**: once *with* your prompt and once *without*. The two results are blended,
  and how strongly depends on the **guidance scale**. So a 50-step image is really **100 forward passes**.
- Unlike an LLM, every step updates the **entire image**, not one piece.

The main knobs you pass with a request:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Prompt** | What the image should show |
| **Negative prompt** | What it should *not* show |
| **Steps** | More = better quality but slower (usually 30–50) |
| **Guidance scale** | Creativity vs. sticking to the prompt (often around 4) |
| **Image size** | Picked from a fixed menu of resolutions |

## How the models have evolved

Modern image models are built on **diffusion transformers**: the same transformer idea as LLMs, but working on patches
of image instead of tokens of text. The pipeline shape has stayed similar, but every component has grown:

| Part | SDXL (2023) | Qwen Image (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Text encoder | Small CLIP model | A full 7B vision-language model |
| Denoiser | Under 4B parameters | 20B parameters |

Using a real LLM as the text encoder is a big reason modern models can render legible text and follow detailed prompts.
The newest research even builds image models *like* LLMs, generating image tokens one at a time.

## Fewer steps = faster

Since the steps are the slow part, one obvious trick is to **use fewer of them**. **Few-step models** are trained to
produce an image in **8 or fewer steps**, making them roughly **80–90% faster**, at some cost in quality. They're usually
made by *distillation* (training a small, fast model to imitate a big one). They're great for things like real-time
filters where speed beats perfection. (Tick the checkbox in the demo above.)

## Video: images, but much bigger

Video models are like image models with an extra dimension: **time**. Early approaches generated one frame, then the
next, and so on, but small errors piled up until the video fell apart. Modern models hold the **entire clip** in
latent space and refine all frames together, with every frame attending to every other frame.

The cost is brutal. Video models have 3–5× more parameters than image models, 10–100× more data in latent space, and
typically need **a whole machine of 8 GPUs working on one video**, taking several seconds per second of footage. The
book compares today's video models to where LLMs were a couple of years ago: slow, sometimes unrealistic, and limited
to short outputs.

<KeyPoints>
- Image generation is a **pipeline**: text encoder → denoiser → VAE.
- It works in compressed **latent space** (~1% of the pixels).
- It starts from noise and refines the **whole image** over ~30–50 steps, with 2 forward passes per step.
- **Few-step** models trade some quality for ~80–90% speedups.
- Video = the same idea across time, and it's extremely compute-hungry.
</KeyPoints>
